Traditional blogs often feel like a series of finished and standalone thoughts. But learning, ideation, and exploration are never truly complete. That’s why I’ve chosen to build a *digital garden* instead of a traditional blog.

### What is a Digital Garden?
A digital garden is a collection of evolving notes, essays, and thoughts that grow over time. Instead of polished, one-off articles, this space will function as an interconnected web of ideas, each note linking to related concepts and insights.

Unlike a blog:
- Notes are **never truly finished** - they evolve with new insights and connections.
- Ideas are **interconnected**, forming a network of knowledge instead of isolated posts.
- Readers are encouraged to **explore** rather than just consume content in a linear way.

### My Approach
I’m using a [**Zettelkasten**](https://zettelkasten.de/introduction/)-inspired method to structure my notes, which means:
- Each note is **atomic** (focusing on a single concept).
- Notes are **linked** together to form a larger picture.
- I continuously **refactor** my notes to improve clarity and usefulness.

### What You’ll Find Here
- **Software Engineering Insights** – Patterns, practices, and lessons I’m learning.
- **Product Craftsmanship** – Thoughts on building great products and teams.
- **Self-Mastery** – Mental models, habits, and principles for continuous improvement.
- **Book Notes & Explorations** – Summaries and personal takes on things I’m reading.

This garden will evolve over time, so feel free to explore and check back for updates. The **Graph View** is a great way to visualize how these ideas connect.
